## Purpose

Short-form is a retention problem wearing a script's clothing. The writing
exists to stop a thumb and then keep earning the next three seconds.

## Rate

180–200 wpm, faster than any other format. At 190 wpm:

| Duration | Words |
|---|---|
| 15s | 48 |
| 30s | 95 |
| 45s | 143 |
| 60s | 190 |

## The first line

It carries most of the outcome. It must do one of:

- Name the exact person watching ("If you edit video on a laptop...")
- State a result and withhold the method
- Contradict something the viewer believes
- Start mid-action, as if they walked in on it

It must not: greet anyone, introduce you, explain what the video will cover,
or say "in this video."

Never open with "Hey guys." Never say "let me explain." Explain instead.

## Retention structure

```text
0–1s     Hook. One sentence. No throat-clearing.
1–3s     Stake. Why keep watching.
3–Xs     Payload in escalating beats. Each beat pays off and opens the next.
Last 3s  Land it. One line. Optional soft CTA.
```

**Open loops**, close them fast. "The third one is the reason most people
quit" only works if the third one arrives within fifteen seconds.

**Cut every transition word.** "So," "basically," "now," "anyway," and "alright"
cost tenths of a second each and add nothing.

## Delivery

Short-form reads are energetic but not shouted. Direction that works:
`[quickly]` on setup, a hard stop before the reveal, `[flat]` for a dry punch.
Fast and uniform is exhausting; the pace has to vary or attention flattens.
