# Louis Kahn Reference Card

Use this card only when the user requests Louis Kahn or Kahn as a reference architect. Do not say "Kahn would design it this way." Translate observed principles into questions and drawing operations for the user's own proposal.

Sources checked: University of Pennsylvania Architectural Archives Louis I. Kahn Collection page, MoMA Richards Medical Research Building page, Louis Kahn "Architecture Is the Thoughtful Making of Spaces" as republished from Perspecta 4, and Docomomo Journal issue summary on Kahn and permanence. Checked on 2026-08-12.

## Principle: Served And Servant Spaces

### Observation

MoMA explains the Richards Medical Research Building through Kahn's distinction between served spaces and servant spaces, with service towers enabling flexible laboratory areas.

### Design question

Are the main spaces and supporting spaces clearly ordered, or are storage, stairs, equipment, and toilets weakening the primary rooms?

### Drawing check

Mark main rooms as served spaces and stairs, bathrooms, storage, shafts, and service routes as servant spaces. Check whether each has an appropriate structure and position.

### Possible operation

Redraw the plan in two colors: served spaces and servant spaces. Consolidate one scattered service element so the main space becomes more legible.

### Sources

- MoMA, "Louis I. Kahn. Alfred Newton Richards Medical Research Building".
- Louis Kahn, "Architecture Is the Thoughtful Making of Spaces", Perspecta 4, 1957.

## Principle: Order Before Shape

### Observation

Penn's archive profile describes Kahn's mature work through pure geometry, historical depth, and material texture. Kahn's Perspecta text links space, order, structure, and services.

### Design question

What rule organizes the plan before room names are added: grid, bay, court, axis, served/servant bands, or repeated spatial units?

### Drawing check

Check whether walls, columns, openings, stairs, and room boundaries share a legible geometric rule.

### Possible operation

Draw the plan once with only regulating lines and structural/spatial bays. Move one room boundary so it belongs to the rule rather than interrupting it.

### Sources

- University of Pennsylvania Architectural Archives, "Kahn, Louis I."
- Louis Kahn, "Architecture Is the Thoughtful Making of Spaces", Perspecta 4, 1957.

## Principle: Structure And Space Should Explain Each Other

### Observation

Kahn's Perspecta text argues that a space shows how it is made, and MoMA describes Richards as massing and structure articulated around served and servant parts.

### Design question

Can the user understand how the main space is carried by looking at the room, or is structure hidden as an afterthought?

### Drawing check

Check columns, bearing walls, spans, beams, thickened service zones, and whether the section clarifies the primary room.

### Possible operation

Draw a structural bay diagram over the plan and one cross-section through the main space, showing what carries the span and where services sit.

### Sources

- Louis Kahn, "Architecture Is the Thoughtful Making of Spaces", Perspecta 4, 1957.
- MoMA, "Louis I. Kahn. Alfred Newton Richards Medical Research Building".

## Principle: Light As Form-Maker

### Observation

Kahn repeatedly tied light, space, and structure in his writings; the Perspecta text discusses light as part of how spaces are served and characterized.

### Design question

Does the light simply enter through leftover windows, or does it define the room's center, edge, hierarchy, or place of use?

### Drawing check

Check top light, thick wall openings, reading/work positions, deep reveals, and whether light arrives from a direction that supports the room's purpose.

### Possible operation

Choose one main room and draw a section where the opening, wall thickness, and occupied place are designed as one light condition.

### Sources

- Louis Kahn, "Architecture Is the Thoughtful Making of Spaces", Perspecta 4, 1957.
- University of Pennsylvania Architectural Archives, "Kahn, Louis I."

## Principle: Permanence Without Size

### Observation

Docomomo commentary frames Kahn through permanence, tectonic character, materiality, and monumentality. For review use, treat monumentality as civic weight and clarity, not as making a project large.

### Design question

What part of this proposal should feel durable, calm, and public in meaning, even if the project is small?

### Drawing check

Check entry threshold, main room proportion, material mass, structural rhythm, and whether the project has one stable civic or communal anchor.

### Possible operation

Identify one room or wall that carries the project's long-term meaning. Give it clearer proportion, structure, and light rather than adding decorative weight.

### Sources

- Docomomo Journal 58, "Louis I. Kahn - The Permanence".
- University of Pennsylvania Architectural Archives, "Kahn, Louis I."
