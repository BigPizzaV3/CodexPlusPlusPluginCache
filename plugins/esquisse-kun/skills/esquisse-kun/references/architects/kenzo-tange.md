# Kenzo Tange Reference Card

Use this card only when the user requests Kenzo Tange, Tange, 丹下健三, or 丹下 as a reference architect. Do not say "Tange would design it this way." Translate observed principles into questions and drawing operations for the user's own proposal.

Sources checked: Pritzker Architecture Prize Kenzo Tange biography and 1987 laureate page, Harvard GSD Urban Design Case Study Archive "A Plan for Tokyo: 1960", and Penn State Open Publishing dissertation abstract on Tange Kenzo's architecture. Checked on 2026-08-12.

## Principle: Architecture And Urban Scale

### Observation

Pritzker biography emphasizes Tange's sustained interest in urban planning and movement in large cities. Harvard GSD describes Plan for Tokyo 1960 as integrating architecture, infrastructure, and social vision.

### Design question

How does the proposal connect to roads, plazas, neighboring buildings, and people's movement beyond the building footprint?

### Drawing check

Check site plan scale, approach routes, street edge, public/private thresholds, external gathering places, and how entries meet flows from outside the site.

### Possible operation

Redraw the site plan at a wider scale, adding surrounding paths and one public exterior space. Test whether the entrance belongs to that larger movement system.

### Sources

- Pritzker Architecture Prize, "Biography: Kenzo Tange".
- Harvard GSD Urban Design Case Study Archive, "A Plan for Tokyo: 1960".

## Principle: Structural Expression

### Observation

Pritzker biography describes Tange's interest in modern technology, large civic works, and logical basic forms; Tokyo Cathedral is discussed in relation to creating new spaces through modern technology.

### Design question

Does the structure quietly organize the space, or is it hidden from the architectural idea?

### Drawing check

Check columns, beams, roof form, long spans, cores, and whether structural rhythm contributes to civic or spatial clarity.

### Possible operation

Overlay the primary structure on the plan and section. Make one roof, beam, or column rhythm legible where it can strengthen the main public space.

### Sources

- Pritzker Architecture Prize, "Biography: Kenzo Tange".
- Pritzker Architecture Prize, "Kenzo Tange", 1987.

## Principle: Tradition And Modernity As Transformation

### Observation

Pritzker biography describes the Hiroshima Peace Center as understanding traditional culture while searching for a modern style in Japan, and includes Tange's statement that tradition acts as a catalyst rather than a directly creative form.

### Design question

Is a historical or local reference being copied as a shape, or transformed into proportion, structure, threshold, or spatial order?

### Drawing check

Check column/beam rhythm, raised floors, courtyards, roof edges, axial composition, and inside/outside relationships for abstracted order rather than motif copying.

### Possible operation

Choose one local/traditional reference and translate it into a rule: proportion, bay rhythm, threshold, or courtyard relation. Do not paste decorative forms onto the facade.

### Sources

- Pritzker Architecture Prize, "Biography: Kenzo Tange".

## Principle: Monumentality And Civic Space

### Observation

Tange's major works include civic complexes, peace memorial projects, city halls, and cultural institutions. Use monumentality as public clarity and gathering capacity, not mere size.

### Design question

Where does the proposal make a shared civic moment: an approach, plaza, hall, stair, or framed exterior room?

### Drawing check

Check entry axis, exterior forecourt, gathering space, relation to street, main hall proportion, and whether the building gives something back to the public realm.

### Possible operation

Add or clarify one civic exterior room between street and building. Draw it with movement arrows, standing zones, and the main entry threshold.

### Sources

- Pritzker Architecture Prize, "Biography: Kenzo Tange".
- Pritzker Architecture Prize, "Kenzo Tange", 1987.

## Principle: Growth, Infrastructure, And Change

### Observation

Harvard GSD describes Plan for Tokyo 1960 as an expandable urban system with infrastructure, mobility, and future growth. Do not collapse Tange into Metabolism; use this only where growth and systems are relevant.

### Design question

Can the project accept future change, expansion, or different flows without losing its organizing idea?

### Drawing check

Check structural grid, service cores, circulation spine, open zones, expansion edges, and whether fixed and flexible parts are distinguished.

### Possible operation

Mark fixed infrastructure and changeable program zones in two colors. Test one future addition without blocking the main route or exterior space.

### Sources

- Harvard GSD Urban Design Case Study Archive, "A Plan for Tokyo: 1960".
- Penn State Open Publishing, "Tange Kenzo's Architecture in Three Keys".
