# Tadao Ando Reference Card

Use this card only when the user requests Tadao Ando, Ando, 安藤忠雄, or 安藤 as a reference architect. Do not say "Ando would design it this way." Translate observed principles into questions and drawing operations for the user's own proposal.

Sources checked: Pritzker Architecture Prize 1995 laureate page, TADAO ANDO | YOUTH exhibition page, ArchDaily Church of the Light page, and Whitewall interview page metadata/search result. Checked on 2026-08-12. The Whitewall page timed out during fetch, so use it only as a source candidate, not as the basis for specific claims.

## Principle: Geometry And Spatial Order

### Observation

The Pritzker page includes Ando's own statement about creating architectural order from geometry and cites commentary on rectangular systems cut by curved or angled walls.

### Design question

What simple geometric rule gives the proposal discipline, and where does a wall, court, or stair intentionally interrupt it?

### Drawing check

Check axes, rectangles, circles, diagonals, wall alignments, and whether geometry supports human occupation rather than remaining abstract.

### Possible operation

Draw the plan with only primary geometry and one intentional cut. Remove or align secondary walls that do not strengthen the spatial order.

### Sources

- Pritzker Architecture Prize, "Tadao Ando", 1995.

## Principle: Light And Darkness As Spatial Contrast

### Observation

Pritzker commentary describes Ando's restraint as focusing attention on volumes, light on walls, and processional sequences. Church of the Light commentary discusses light/dark and solid/void dualities.

### Design question

Where should the project be dark enough for light to become active, and where is brightness only making the plan flat?

### Drawing check

Check opening size, wall surface receiving light, dark entry zones, courtyard edges, and whether a section explains the contrast.

### Possible operation

Make two section sketches: one with direct bright entry and one with a darker approach before the main light. Compare which supports the concept.

### Sources

- Pritzker Architecture Prize, "Tadao Ando", 1995.
- ArchDaily, "AD Classics: Church of the Light / Tadao Ando Architect & Associates".

## Principle: Wall As Spatial Device

### Observation

The Pritzker page includes Ando's statement that walls can divide space, transform place, and create new domains.

### Design question

Is a wall only enclosing rooms, or is it shaping approach, controlling views, and making a new relation between inside and outside?

### Drawing check

Check long walls, diagonal or offset walls, blind walls, view stops, courtyard walls, and where the route turns because of a wall.

### Possible operation

Add one test wall overlay from entrance toward the main space. Compare a direct view to the courtyard with a delayed view after a turn.

### Sources

- Pritzker Architecture Prize, "Tadao Ando", 1995.

## Principle: Sequence And Approach

### Observation

Pritzker commentary notes processional sequences in Ando's work. Treat this as an observed design tendency, not a rule that every project must imitate.

### Design question

Does the approach create a deliberate transition from city/site to interior, or does the user arrive too directly without spatial preparation?

### Drawing check

Check entrance position, turns, compression, exterior-to-interior threshold, and first view of the main room or courtyard.

### Possible operation

Draw an approach diagram with three moments: outside, controlled threshold, and release. Test one alternate route that bends once before entry.

### Sources

- Pritzker Architecture Prize, "Tadao Ando", 1995.
- ArchDaily, "AD Classics: Church of the Light / Tadao Ando Architect & Associates".

## Principle: Nature As Constructed Relationship

### Observation

The Pritzker page includes Ando's statement about restoring unity between house and nature through light and wind, using forces in the area where he builds.

### Design question

Which natural condition is the design actively framing: light, wind, sky, water, slope, or seasonal change?

### Drawing check

Check courtyard openings, sky views, wind paths, water edges, topography, and whether the building frames nature rather than treating it as decoration.

### Possible operation

Choose one natural condition and draw a plan/section pair showing how a wall or court frames it. Avoid solving this by changing the finish to exposed concrete.

### Sources

- Pritzker Architecture Prize, "Tadao Ando", 1995.
